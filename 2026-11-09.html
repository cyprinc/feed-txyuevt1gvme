台湾半导体分析师陆行之（Andrew Lu）对黄仁勋丑闻的看法：
美国为何借丑闻拔刀：三个战略目的
这桩丑闻的目的绝不是揭露一段婚外情，而是要强行推进三个关键目标——这些目标在中美科技战中一直因黄仁勋的反对而毫无进展。
第一刀：彻底摧毁黄仁勋在华盛顿反制裁游说的正当性
过去两年，硅谷反对全面禁止对华芯片出口声音最大、影响力最强的企业领袖就是黄仁勋。他一直对白宫说同样的话：“把中国市场让给华为，最后死掉的只会是美国科技产业。”
但一旦美国情报部门把被点名为黄仁勋婚外情对象的女子定性为“为中国国家安全部工作、试图推动美国放松芯片管制的间谍”，他为放宽管制所做的每一分游说都会被赋予完全不同的政治含义。这就变成了国家安全问题——外国间谍可能利用秘密关系施加影响。
黄仁勋再也无法在国会或白宫为中国市场说一句话。如果他说了，就会被扣上“中了情报圈套还没醒悟”的帽子。
第二刀：推翻对华 H200 出口许可，切断中国空壳公司与东南亚走私网络的联系
看看这两篇报道的时间点。
11 月 8 日，也就是黄仁勋丑闻爆出的同一天，《纽约时报》发表了一篇调查报道：记者亲自走访了数十家获美国商务部批准经手英伟达 H200 芯片的中国公司，北京的新矩阵（New Matrix）就是其中之一。报道称，获准购买芯片的公司的注册地址竟是空无一人的“幽灵办公室”，其中一些公司与美国制裁名单上的实体以及与中国军方有合同关系的企业关系密切。
一篇爆料说，英伟达 CEO 与负责芯片走私的新加坡公司 Megaspeed 的一名女间谍有染；另一篇说，商务部批准购买 H200 的中国公司不过是替中国军方交易的空壳公司。这两篇报道的目的很明确：向特朗普政府和商务部施压，全面撤销或冻结对华 H200 出口许可，并进一步迫使当局彻底整改通过新加坡等东南亚地区第三方运营的数据中心转移芯片的漏洞。
第三刀：通过国会监督和独立调查给英伟达套上国家安全的缰绳
作为全球市值最大的公司和 AI 领域的主导力量，英伟达此前在经营上享有广泛的自主权。但这一事件之后，负责中国、外交和情报事务的三个国会委员会已开始直接审查英伟达内部的安全措施，甚至迫使英伟达董事会成立独立调查委员会，调查公司自己的创始人兼 CEO。
实际上，华盛顿的国家安全体系已经在英伟达董事会内部安插了一只看门狗。从今往后，目标是确保一切——细到英伟达每一颗高性能芯片的最终去向——都严格符合美国国家安全战略。这也意味着，打擦边球、专门针对监管门槛定制规格来发布芯片的空间将不复存在。
结论
“丑闻是一枚准时引爆的雷管，真正的目的是堵住芯片外流的漏洞。”
黄仁勋犯了一个致命错误：在私生活和人际关系上放松了警惕。最终他落入了情报战的教科书式手段——美人计，并因此被拿住把柄。美国国家安全和情报机构毫不留情地利用了他私生活暴露的弱点，结果一举解决了三个棘手问题：英伟达在白宫游说放松对华限制、H200 芯片流入中国空壳公司，以及经由新加坡的走私。
定于 11 月 18 日举行的国会闭门听证会，将是美国认真着手进一步收紧对中国 AI 算力制裁的决定性转折点。

原文：Taiwanese semiconductor analyst Andrew Lu’s take on the recent scandal involving Jensen Huang:
Why the US Seized on the Scandal to Draw Its Sword: Three Strategic Objectives
The purpose of this scandal is by no means to expose an affair. It is to force through three critical objectives that had made no headway in the tech war between the US and China because Jensen Huang opposed them.
The first cut: Completely destroy the legitimacy of Jensen Huang's lobbying against sanctions in Washington
Over the past two years, the loudest and most influential business leader in Silicon Valley opposing a total ban on chip exports to China has been Jensen Huang. He has told the White House the same thing all along: "Cede the Chinese market to Huawei, and the only one that ends up dead is the American tech industry."
But the moment US intelligence labels the woman named as Huang's affair partner "a spy working for China's Ministry of State Security who sought to steer the US toward easing its chip controls," every bit of lobbying he has done for looser controls takes on an entirely different political meaning. It becomes a national security matter, raising the possibility that a foreign spy used a secret relationship to exert influence.
Huang can no longer say a single word in defense of the Chinese market before Congress or the White House. If he does, he will be branded as someone who "fell for an intelligence operation and still hasn't come to his senses."
The second cut: Reverse the H200 export approvals for China and sever the link between Chinese shell companies and Southeast Asian smuggling networks
Look at the timing of the two reports.
On November 8, the very day the Jensen Huang scandal broke, The New York Times published an investigation for which its reporters went in person to dozens of Chinese companies that the US Commerce Department had approved to handle Nvidia's H200 chips. New Matrix in Beijing was one of them. According to the report, the registered addresses of companies approved to buy the chips turned out to be empty "ghost offices," and some of the companies had close ties to entities on US sanctions lists and to firms under contract with the Chinese military.
One exposé says Nvidia's CEO was having an affair with a female spy from Megaspeed, the Singapore company that handled the chip smuggling. The other says the Chinese companies Commerce approved to buy H200s are nothing but shell companies trading on behalf of the Chinese military. The aim of these two reports is clear. They are meant to pressure the Trump administration and the Commerce Department into fully revoking or freezing H200 export approvals for China and, going further, to force a sweeping overhaul of the loophole that let chips be diverted through data centers run by third parties in Singapore and elsewhere in Southeast Asia.
The third cut: Put Nvidia on a national security leash through congressional oversight and an independent investigation
As the world's largest company by market cap and the dominant force in AI, Nvidia has until now enjoyed broad autonomy in how it runs its business. But in the wake of this incident, the three congressional committees responsible for China, foreign affairs, and intelligence have begun examining Nvidia's internal security measures directly. They have even pressured Nvidia's board into forming an independent investigation committee to look into the company's own founder and CEO.
In effect, Washington's national security establishment has planted a watchdog right inside Nvidia's board. From now on, the goal is to make sure that everything, down to where each and every one of Nvidia's high performance chips ends up, conforms strictly to US national security strategy. It also means there is no more room for skirting the edge of the rules or releasing chips with specs specially tailored to regulatory thresholds.
Conclusion
"The scandal was a detonator set off right on cue. The real goal was to plug the holes chips were leaking through."
Jensen Huang made the fatal mistake of letting his guard down in his private life and personal relationships. In the end, he fell into a honey trap, the textbook tactic of intelligence warfare, and was compromised. America's national security and intelligence agencies ruthlessly exploited the weakness his private life had exposed. As a result, they solved three thorny problems in a single stroke: Nvidia's lobbying at the White House to ease restrictions on China, the flow of H200 chips into Chinese shell companies, and smuggling routed through Singapore.
The closed congressional hearing set for November 18 will be the decisive turning point at which the US moves in earnest to tighten its sanctions on China's AI compute even further.
