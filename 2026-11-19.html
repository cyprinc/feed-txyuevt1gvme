In August, $NVDA announced a partnership with Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilize $500B to help customers like OpenAI, Anthropic and smaller AI cloud companies finance chip purchases.
But the agreement was only a few-page memorandum of understanding. None of the firms was obligated to raise any money, and no capital has been deployed.

As lenders have become more cautious about AI financing, they have pushed Nvidia to offer greater protection.
Nvidia has also been pulling back its financial guarantees. Earlier, reports of a proposed $250B Nvidia backstop for an OpenAI data center sent $NVDA shares down 5%, prompting Nvidia to cut its initial commitment in half.
Interim CEO Colette Kress has since signaled that future investments and guarantees will be evaluated more strictly.
Talks on the partnership's first chip-backed financing deal are still underway, but nothing is expected to close before 2027.
Apollo CEO Marc Rowan warned that without Nvidia providing financial guarantees, a large number of neocloud companies could lose access to capital.

原文：TSMC & SAMSUNG SUSPEND SOME CHIP SHIPMENTS TO CHINA 🇨🇳 AFTER NEW U.S. EXPORT CONTROLS: FT
Both chipmakers have halted deliveries of certain processors to Chinese customers while they apply for licenses from the U.S. Commerce Department.
The new rules close a route Chinese chip designers previously used to get advanced AI chips manufactured by TSMC and Samsung through independent packaging and testing firms without direct U.S. approval.
The restrictions also extend to consumer chips made using advanced manufacturing processes, including smartphone processors. Both companies have suspended some shipments to consumer electronics customers.
Samsung's Chinese clients include Baidu's Kunlun AI unit and ByteDance, while TSMC manufactures smartphone chips for Xiaomi, Oppo and Vivo.
U.S. licenses for consumer chips are expected to be granted, but the approval process could cause significant manufacturing delays.
