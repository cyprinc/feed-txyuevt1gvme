关于华为在 ISSCC 上发布的 HBM 工作的几点想法。
我把它看作华为以类似 Tau 的方式绕开 EUV 瓶颈。中国终于绕过 JEDEC，走自己的路了。
如果这真的可行，华为可以在 HBM3 级别的引脚速率下实现 HBM4 级别的单堆栈带宽。没有 EUV 级别的 DRAM 微缩，就很难进一步提升 HBM 引脚速率。所以华为干脆走了一条完全不同的路线。
晶圆级混合键合也是中国的强项。（长江存储也采用晶圆对晶圆，即 W2W 混合键合。）
我觉得特别有意思的是，包括三星在内的西方生态系统中的 HBM 厂商，会用台积电 3nm 这样的先进代工节点来做逻辑 die，以实现高引脚速率。华为在这两方面都受到限制：它的 DRAM 微缩走不了那么远，也拿不到先进的代工节点。所以它选择做得更宽，以弥补这些工艺上的限制。
当然，如果事情这么简单，三星早就这么做了。W2W 的一大缺点是无法在堆叠前剔除单个有缺陷的 die，因此各层的良率会相乘。
假设每层良率为 95%，堆叠五层后良率就降到 77%。
而 95% 已经是相当乐观的假设了。如果每层良率是 70%，五层堆叠的良率就只剩 17%。那简直是灾难。
更痛苦的是，这会吃掉中国本就有限的 DRAM 晶圆供应。晶粒对晶圆（D2W）键合可以在堆叠前挑选已知良品 die。对于五层堆叠，W2W 每个良品堆叠所用的晶圆数是 D2W 的 1/y⁴ 倍，其中 y 为每层良率。在堆叠良率为 77% 时，W2W 用的晶圆是 D2W 的 1.23 倍；在 17% 时，则跃升到惊人的 4.16 倍。
换句话说，在那种情形下，中国制造一个 HBM 堆叠所需的晶圆要比 SK 海力士多四倍以上。这意味着中国需要韩国和美光合计 DRAM 产能的四倍以上，才能匹配美国 AI 加速器的出货量。（这是最坏的情形。）
不过，这仍是一种非常有意思的思路。感觉中国和西方的技术标准正在逐渐分化，就像冷战时期苏联和西方的标准那样。

原文：A few thoughts on Huawei’s HBM work at ISSCC.
I see it as Huawei’s Tau-style way around the EUV bottleneck. China is finally bypassing JEDEC and going its own way.
If this actually works, Huawei could get HBM4-class bandwidth per stack at HBM3-class pin speeds. Without EUV-class DRAM scaling, it’s hard to push HBM pin speeds higher. So Huawei is taking a different route altogether.
Wafer-scale hybrid bonding is also an area where China is strong. (YMTC uses wafer-to-wafer, or W2W, hybrid bonding too.)
What I find especially interesting is that HBM makers in the Western ecosystem, including Samsung, use leading-edge foundry nodes like TSMC’s 3nm for their logic dies to hit high pin speeds. Huawei is constrained on both fronts. It can’t scale its DRAM as far, and it doesn’t have access to leading-edge foundry nodes. So it’s going wider to make up for those process limitations.
Of course, if it were that simple, Samsung would have done it already. The big drawback with W2W is that you can’t exclude individual defective dies before stacking, so the yields of the individual layers multiply.
Say each layer has a 95% yield. Stack five layers and the yield drops to 77%.
And 95% is already a pretty optimistic assumption. At 70% per layer, the yield for a five-layer stack drops to just 17%. That’s a disaster.
What makes this even more painful is that it eats into China’s already limited supply of DRAM wafers. Die-to-wafer (D2W) bonding lets you select known-good dies before stacking. For a five-layer stack, W2W uses 1/y⁴ times as many wafers per good stack as D2W, where y is the yield per layer. At a stack yield of 77%, W2W uses 1.23× as many wafers as D2W. At 17%, that jumps to a whopping 4.16×.
In other words, in that scenario, China would need more than four times as many wafers as SK hynix to make a single HBM stack. That would mean China needs more than four times the combined DRAM production capacity of South Korea and Micron to match U.S. AI accelerator shipments. (That’s the worst-case scenario.)
Still, it’s a fascinating approach. It feels like Chinese and Western tech standards are gradually diverging, much like Soviet and Western standards did during the Cold War.

有的时候我觉得，这已经不是赚钱与否、是否创造一个产品、享受乐趣这么简单的一件事。
于我而言，谁不想一起创造一个我们前所未见的、从亘古到现在都没有出现过的伟大时代呢？这样伟大的时代，不可能，也不应该仅仅属于硅谷。
