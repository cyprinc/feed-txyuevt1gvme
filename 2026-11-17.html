根据新规，中国及其他受限地区的芯片设计公司，除非获得 BIS 认定的“批准”或“授权”IC 设计商身份，否则不能再通过现有的晶体管数量核验流程获得豁免。按照此前的规定，晶圆代工厂或封测（OSAT）厂商可以核验晶体管数量来确定豁免资格。现在则必须由 BIS 自己决定是否给予豁免。

原文：Re: The new BIS rule
I missed a provision yesterday.
Under the new rule, chip designers in China and other restricted regions can no longer qualify for an exemption through the existing transistor-count verification process unless they have BIS status as an “approved” or “authorized” IC designer. Under the previous rule, a foundry or an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) provider could verify the transistor count to establish eligibility for an exemption. Now, BIS itself has to decide whether to grant one.
As a result, foundries such as TSMC and Samsung Electronics, as well as OSAT providers such as ASE and Amkor, will need to conduct more thorough background checks on their customers.

现在市政府正在解散这支专业部队，引入不携带武器的平民降级处置小组，并限制警察可用的手段。
我支持降级处置。每一位负责任的警察都支持。但降级处置是一种策略，而不能取代应对暴力的能力。
你没法跟砸向店面的砖头讲道理。你没法跟燃烧瓶谈判。你也不能派一名社工去做受过专门训练的警察面对暴徒时该做的事。
纽约警察局仍会派警员应对抗议。但市政府一直没能回答的问题是：没有一支专门针对这类危险局面训练的部队，纽约人为什么会更安全？
当和平抗议者遭到袭击时怎么办？当商户被破坏时怎么办？当警察被包围和袭击时怎么办？
谁来应对？多快能到？受过什么训练？用什么手段？
这些不是假设性的问题。正是这些决定，决定了一个危险局面能被控制住，还是整个街区陷入混乱。
我当了四年市长，在纽约警察局干了 22 年。我知道保护一个人的抗议权利，和允许有人以抗议为掩护实施暴力之间的区别。
抗议的权利不包括制造恐怖的权利。保护公民自由也永远不应以牺牲公共安全为代价。
市政府也许认为解散 SRG 是一场政治胜利。
但如果暴力爆发，付出代价的不会是为这一宣布鼓掌的活动人士，也不会是在市政厅庆祝的政客。
而将是遭到袭击的警察，是生计被毁的小企业主，是被卷入暴力的和平抗议者，是只想平安回家的无辜纽约人。
纽约市的公共安全不是意识形态实验的试验场。我们的警察不是可以牺牲的。公共安全也不是可以拿来博取政治分数的赌注。
市政府应在纽约人以惨痛方式发现后果之前撤回这一决定。

原文：There is a reason New York City has not experienced the kind of violent riots we have witnessed in Paris and other major cities around the world.
We had the NYPD’s Strategic Response Group (SRG), a specialized unit of highly trained officers prepared to confront violence before it spiraled out of control.
Let me make something absolutely clear.
Peaceful protest is a constitutional right. Violent rioting is a crime. And confusing the two is a dangerous failure of leadership.
The SRG was not created to silence peaceful protesters. It was created to protect New Yorkers when demonstrations crossed the line into violence, to protect businesses from destruction, peaceful demonstrators from violent agitators, and police officers from being assaulted.
Now City Hall is dismantling that specialized unit, introducing unarmed civilian de-escalation teams, and restricting the tools available to police.
I support de-escalation. Every responsible police officer does. But de-escalation is a strategy, not a substitute for the ability to confront violence.
You cannot reason with a brick flying through a storefront. You cannot negotiate with a Molotov cocktail. And you cannot send a social worker to do the job of a specially trained police officer confronting a violent mob.
The NYPD will still have officers responding to protests. But the question City Hall has failed to answer is why New Yorkers will be safer without a dedicated unit specifically trained for these dangerous situations.
What happens when peaceful protesters are attacked? When businesses are being destroyed? When police officers are surrounded and assaulted?
Who responds? How quickly? With what training? And with what tools?
These are not hypothetical questions. These are the decisions that determine whether a dangerous situation is contained or an entire neighborhood descends into chaos.
I spent four years as mayor and 22 years as an NYPD officer. I know the difference between protecting someone’s right to protest and allowing someone to use that protest as cover for violence.
The right to protest does not include the right to terrorize. And protecting civil liberties should never require surrendering public safety.
City Hall may believe dismantling the SRG is a political victory.
But if violence erupts, the people paying the price won’t be the activists applauding this announcement or the politicians celebrating it at City Hall.
It will be the police officer who gets assaulted. The small-business owner whose livelihood is destroyed. The peaceful protester who gets caught in the violence. The innocent New Yorker who simply wants to get home safely.
New York City’s Public Safety is not a laboratory for ideological experiments. Our police officers are not expendable. And public safety is not something you gamble with to score political points.
City Hall should reverse this decision before New Yorkers discover the consequences the hard way.
